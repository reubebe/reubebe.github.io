02 / About

Clinical questions first. Computation in service of the evidence.

I came into quantitative science through clinical and movement-disorders research. That still shapes how I work: start with the decision a clinician, investigator, or study team needs to make, then build the study design, statistical framework, and computational workflow around it.

That has included Parkinson’s disease studies, EEG and neuroimaging pipelines, longitudinal treatment and behavioral outcomes, Bayesian trial-design systems, and biomedical machine learning. I am especially interested in work where uncertainty, provenance, reproducibility, and interpretation determine how strong the evidence really is.

I currently lead the Motor Control Lab at DePauw University and work with the Chinese American Biopharmaceutical Society’s data-science program. I completed Georgia Tech’s M.S. in Analytics, Computational Data Analytics Track in 2026, building on a Ph.D. in Kinesiology focused on motor behavior and neuroscience.

OpenTrial / project preview
Bayesian trial design · computation engine

OpenTrial

A learning-oriented trial-design report engine built around one question: can the design inputs, priors, and operating characteristics be traced back to evidence and checked independently?

System shape

InputsIndication, endpoint, effect, SD or baseline event rate, alpha, power, max N, dropout and advanced design settings.
EvidenceClinicalTrials.gov, PubMed, openFDA, DailyMed plus translational and literature sources when enabled.
OutputsEvidence-derived priors, power/beta/assurance, simulation, prior sensitivity, decision criteria, sequential boundaries, and downloadable reports.
InterfaceVanilla JavaScript workspace backed by the same Python statistical engine; original Streamlit interface remains available.

What makes it interesting

The interface is secondary to the audit trail. Evidence records without a usable effect and standard error can remain visible for provenance while being excluded from prior construction. Monte Carlo operating characteristics expose empirical Type I error as a calibration check, and an independent SciPy/statsmodels validation path re-derives core quantities.

The design workspace covers continuous and binary endpoints, prior sensitivity, Bayesian decision criteria, group-sequential designs, REML heterogeneity, dropout adjustment, duplicate-trial de-duplication, PDF export, and an audit mode for comparing an existing trial with the engine recommendation.

Evidence boundary

OpenTrial is explicitly a learning-oriented computation engine, not a validated clinical tool. The portfolio keeps that qualification visible because methodological honesty is part of the project’s value.

KinetiScan / project preview
Markerless movement analysis · local research instrument

KinetiScan

A research system for recovering three-dimensional joint kinematics from ordinary video, with monocular and N-view pathways feeding a parity-checked clinical kinematics engine and a hash-linked evidence bundle.

Two pathways, one engine

1 cameraMediaPipe rays + camera distance + standing height feed an ONNX transformer that emits 20 metric 3D joints. This pathway is labelled exploratory.
2–64 camsExplicit synchronization, shared-world calibration, confidence-weighted N-view DLT and filtering produce geometric 3D joint coordinates.
EngineJoint angles, smoothing, dynamic-dt velocity, gait events, ROM, symmetry, variability and gait-phase outputs share one authoritative kinematics engine.
PrivacyBrowser-selected video is processed on the analyst’s own machine; the workflow does not require cloud upload.

Validation is separated from reconstruction

The project deliberately distinguishes validation of the kinematics engine from validation of the complete camera-to-kinematics pathway. Published-in-repo agreement numbers were produced by applying the engine to laboratory marker coordinates; they do not establish end-to-end camera validity.

Evidence tiers are enforced in code: withheld when required evidence is missing, exploratory for raw-video pathways without end-to-end validation, and trusted only for prepared observations carrying capture-system evidence. Monocular ankle magnitude is withheld because the pathway does not independently recover foot depth.

Next decisive experiment

A prospective, participant-disjoint, convention-matched evaluation of the complete video-to-kinematics path has not yet been run. Pathological gait, operator effects, and uncontrolled environments also remain untested.
