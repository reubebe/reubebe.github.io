About

I am a data scientist and quantitative researcher who likes hard, messy problems in human health. Over seven years I have designed experimental and observational studies, built statistical and machine learning models, and turned complicated results into decisions that clinicians, researchers, and teams can use. My work sits where applied machine learning meets Bayesian statistics, causal inference, and behavioral and neurophysiological science.

I am especially drawn to building reproducible analytical tools for healthcare, biopharma, clinical research, and human performance. Right now I am a data science intern with the Chinese American Biopharmaceutical Society, I direct the Motor Control Lab at DePauw University, and I recently finished an M.S. in Analytics at Georgia Tech.

Selected work

OpenTrial · Bayesian trial design engine

A computation engine that turns structured trial inputs into a cited, reproducible design report. It pulls evidence-derived priors with source-level provenance from ClinicalTrials.gov, PubMed, openFDA, and DailyMed, then produces power grids, prior-sensitivity analysis, predictive probability-of-success criteria, and simulation-calibrated group-sequential designs. Built during my internship with the Chinese American Biopharmaceutical Society. View repository.

Python · Streamlit · Bayesian inference · simulation · clinical data APIs

ECG signal quality classification

A deep-learning pipeline for cardiac signal quality on the PTB-XL benchmark: a CNN-Transformer hybrid ensemble against L1-regularized logistic regression, MLP, and CNN baselines, with stratified cross-validation, Youden-index threshold optimization, and saliency and per-lead importance analysis end to end. View repository.

Python · PyTorch · deep learning · signal processing · interpretability

Domain background

My research grounding is in neurological movement disorders, with a focus on Parkinson's disease. During my Ph.D. at Louisiana State University I ran longitudinal studies of motor and cognitive outcomes and built EEG signal-processing pipelines. As a postdoctoral fellow in the Brain Recovery Lab at the MGH Institute of Health Professions, I applied multilevel mixed-effects models to longitudinal fMRI and TMS datasets for NIH-funded studies. Earlier, a Mitacs internship at Memorial University put me on a clinical feasibility study of a remote tele-ultrasound system, later published in the Journal of Ultrasound in Medicine.
